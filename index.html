    Proration, billing schedules, and extended-amount math for contract amendments across a Salesforce ↔ Workday integration.

    Amendments are where billing gets strange: add-ons, rip-and-replace lines, zero-credit cancellations, upgrades and downgrades mid-term, multi-year terms, more than one currency.   3. Plate TK Reviewer output / demo capture to be added

    An internal AI reviewer that reads changes to billing-calculator code and flags financial-math bugs.

    Built as part of Hudl's Claude Code experimentation initiative. It looks for floating-point types on money paths, installment splits that don't reconcile to their total, inconsistent rounding modes, unrounded division on persisted amounts, date-boundary errors, and missing exact-amount tests. The rules are grounded in the actual codebase rather than in generic advice about money.

    For the demo I planted a real billing bug and let the reviewer find it. Merged, along with a proposal to look at the approach more widely.

    I like this one because the AI is doing something specific and checkable, which is the only version of it I find interesting.

AboutHouston → Philadelphia → Nebraska

Computer science, then graphics, then production software — in that order, and none of it left behind.

I started in computer science at Bryn Mawr, went sideways into computer graphics and game technology at Penn, and came back around to building software people actually depend on.

Those aren't separate skills for me. Graphics taught me to care about correctness at the level of a single float, and that a thing also has to look right, not only compute right. Billing taught me the same lesson with money attached: a rounding mode is an aesthetic decision right up until someone gets the wrong invoice.

Mostly I just like making things and finding out how they work.
